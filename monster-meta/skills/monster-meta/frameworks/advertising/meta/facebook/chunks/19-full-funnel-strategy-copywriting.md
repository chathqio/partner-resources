# Chunk 19: Full-Funnel Strategy & Ad Copywriting
## Source: Perry Marshall (80/20 Sales & Marketing), Eugene Schwartz (Breakthrough Advertising), practitioner consensus (2026)

---

## Core Concept

A full-funnel Meta ads strategy coordinates messaging, creative, and offers across the entire customer journey — from first impression to purchase and beyond. Most advertisers fail not because their ads are bad, but because they run bottom-of-funnel conversion campaigns to ice-cold audiences with no prior exposure. The full-funnel approach builds awareness pools, nurtures consideration, and converts purchase-ready prospects — each stage feeding the next.

Ad copywriting is the bridge between creative and conversion. The right words, in the right sequence, to the right audience at the right awareness level determine whether someone scrolls past or stops, clicks, and buys. Eugene Schwartz's awareness levels (unaware → most aware) map directly to funnel stages and dictate which copy framework to deploy. Perry Marshall's 80/20 principle applies ruthlessly: 80% of your conversions come from 20% of your funnel — the post-click experience and follow-up sequence, not the ad itself.

This chunk provides the complete playbook for mapping funnel stages to campaign architecture and writing copy that converts at each stage.

## Frameworks

### Framework: TOFU / MOFU / BOFU Playbook

**When to use:** At the strategy level before building any campaigns. Map your entire funnel before touching Ads Manager.

| Funnel Stage | Objective | Audience Temp | Creative Type | Offer Type | Key Metrics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TOFU (Top of Funnel)** | Awareness, Reach, Video Views | Cold — broad, LAL, interest-based | Educational content, brand story, problem agitation, entertaining video, founder story | No hard sell — value-first. Blog posts, free tools, viral content, "Did you know?" angles | CPM, Reach, Video Views, Hook Rate (3s), ThruPlay Rate |
| **MOFU (Middle of Funnel)** | Traffic, Engagement, Lead Gen | Warm — video viewers, engagers, site visitors | Social proof, case studies, comparison content, webinar invites, lead magnets, how-to guides | Lead magnets, webinar registrations, free trials, quizzes, downloadable guides | CTR, CPL, Content Engagement, Landing Page View Rate |
| **BOFU (Bottom of Funnel)** | Sales, Conversions | Hot — add-to-cart, checkout initiated, email list, past purchasers | Testimonials, demos, urgency creative, risk reversal, product deep-dives, founder direct-to-camera | Direct purchase, limited-time offers, bundle deals, money-back guarantees, free shipping thresholds | CPA, ROAS, Conversion Rate, AOV |

**Stage transition mechanics:**
- TOFU builds custom audiences (video viewers, page engagers) that become MOFU targeting pools
- MOFU captures leads and builds website custom audiences (visitors, content consumers) that become BOFU targeting pools
- BOFU converts the warmest prospects and feeds post-purchase campaigns (upsells, retention, referral)
- Each stage must generate enough audience volume to feed the next — if TOFU is underfunded, MOFU and BOFU starve

### Framework: Budget Allocation by Funnel Stage

**When to use:** When setting initial budgets or rebalancing an existing account.

**Default allocation (established brand with existing data):**
- **60% Top + Mid Funnel** (prospecting + consideration): 40% TOFU, 20% MOFU
- **40% Bottom Funnel** (conversion + retargeting): 30% BOFU, 10% post-purchase/retention

**Why most advertisers over-invest in BOFU:**
- BOFU campaigns show the best ROAS in isolation, creating a false signal that they are "the best campaigns"
- But BOFU only converts people who were already warmed up — without TOFU/MOFU feeding warm audiences, BOFU pools shrink and CPAs rise
- Over-indexing on BOFU leads to audience exhaustion, frequency spikes, and diminishing returns
- The advertisers who scale profitably are the ones who invest in filling the top of the funnel

**Allocation adjustments by business maturity:**

| Business Stage | TOFU | MOFU | BOFU | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **New brand (0-6 months)** | 60% | 20% | 20% | Need awareness and Pixel data before heavy conversion spend |
| **Growth phase (6-18 months)** | 40% | 25% | 35% | Retargeting pools are growing; shift toward conversion |
| **Established brand (18+ months)** | 35% | 20% | 45% | Large warm audiences; maximize conversion efficiency |
| **Scaling aggressively** | 50% | 20% | 30% | Must constantly refill TOFU to avoid exhausting warm pools |

### Framework: Stage-Specific Creative & Targeting

**When to use:** When briefing creative or setting up ad sets within each funnel stage.

**TOFU Creative Playbook:**
- Video content (60-90 seconds): educational, entertaining, problem-awareness
- Founder/expert story: "Here's what I learned after 10 years in [industry]"
- Pattern interrupts: unexpected visuals, counterintuitive hooks, myth-busting
- Targeting: Broad (no targeting), 1-5% LAL of purchasers, broad interest stacks
- Goal: maximize 3-second hook rate and ThruPlay rate — these build retargeting pools

**MOFU Creative Playbook:**
- Case studies with specific numbers: "How [Client] went from X to Y in Z days"
- Comparison content: "Us vs. the old way" — side-by-side format
- Lead magnet creative: clear value proposition, tangible deliverable preview
- Webinar/event creative: speaker credibility, agenda preview, urgency
- Targeting: 50-95% video viewers, page/post engagers, website visitors (30-90 day), email list lookalikes
- Goal: capture contact information or drive meaningful site engagement

**BOFU Creative Playbook:**
- Customer testimonials: video preferred, screenshot acceptable
- Product demos: show the product solving the exact problem
- Urgency and scarcity: limited-time pricing, enrollment deadlines, bonus expiration
- Risk reversal: money-back guarantee, free trial, "cancel anytime"
- Dynamic Product Ads (DPA): personalized product retargeting from catalog
- Targeting: add-to-cart (7-14 day), checkout initiated, email subscribers, past purchasers (cross-sell/upsell)
- Goal: close the sale with minimum friction

### Framework: PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve) for Meta Ads

**When to use:** Best for MOFU and BOFU copy when the audience is problem-aware but hasn't found a solution. Works across all ad formats.

**Structure:**
1. **Problem:** Name the specific pain point the audience experiences. Be concrete, not abstract. "Spending 3 hours a day on manual data entry" beats "struggling with inefficiency."
2. **Agitate:** Amplify the emotional weight. What happens if the problem continues? What has the audience already tried that failed? Make the status quo feel unbearable.
3. **Solve:** Position your product as the resolution. Transition from pain to relief. Include a specific outcome or proof point.

**PAS adapted by ad format:**

| Format | Problem | Agitate | Solve |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Image ad (primary text)** | Opening line — name the pain | 2-3 sentences expanding consequences | Final lines — introduce product + CTA |
| **Video ad (script)** | First 3 seconds — hook with the problem | Seconds 4-15 — deepen the frustration | Seconds 16-30 — reveal the solution + CTA |
| **Carousel** | Slide 1 — "Tired of [problem]?" | Slides 2-3 — consequences and failed alternatives | Slides 4-5 — solution + proof + CTA |

**PAS example (SaaS):**
> Still manually creating reports every Monday morning? (Problem) That's 4 hours a week — 200+ hours a year — spent copying data between spreadsheets instead of growing your business. And every manual report is one typo away from a bad decision. (Agitate) ReportBot builds your weekly reports automatically in 30 seconds. Accurate. Formatted. In your inbox before your coffee's ready. Start your free trial. (Solve)

### Framework: AIDA for Meta Ads

**When to use:** The most versatile copy framework. Works at every funnel stage when adapted to audience temperature. Best for longer-form copy (primary text) and video scripts.

**Structure mapped to ad anatomy:**

| AIDA Stage | Ad Element | Purpose | Execution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attention** | Hook — first line of copy or first 3 seconds of video | Stop the scroll | Bold claim, surprising stat, provocative question, pattern interrupt |
| **Interest** | Body — expand the premise | Keep them reading/watching | Elaborate on the problem or opportunity. Use specifics, not generalities |
| **Desire** | Proof + transformation — show the outcome | Make them want it | Testimonials, results, before/after, "imagine if..." future pacing |
| **Action** | CTA — final line or end screen | Tell them what to do next | Clear, single action. "Shop now," "Download free guide," "Book your call" |

**AIDA by awareness level (Schwartz):**
- **Unaware:** Lead with Attention heavily — they don't know they have a problem. Use curiosity and entertainment.
- **Problem-aware:** Lead with Interest — they know the problem, show you understand it deeply.
- **Solution-aware:** Lead with Desire — they know solutions exist, prove yours is superior.
- **Product-aware:** Lead with Action — they know your product, give them a reason to act now.
- **Most aware:** Skip to Action — retargeting past visitors/cart abandoners. "Still thinking about it? Here's 10% off."

### Framework: BAB (Before-After-Bridge) for Meta Ads

**When to use:** Best for transformation-based products — coaching, fitness, education, SaaS, personal development. Strongest at MOFU and BOFU where you can paint a vivid contrast.

**Structure:**
1. **Before:** Describe the customer's current painful reality. Be specific and visceral — use their language.
2. **After:** Paint the desired future state. Make it tangible and emotionally compelling.
3. **Bridge:** Your product/service is the path from Before to After.

**BAB example (coaching):**
> **Before:** You're posting on social media every day, getting likes from your mom and three friends, but zero clients. You've invested in courses, bought the templates, followed the gurus — and still can't crack $5K months.
>
> **After:** Imagine waking up to 3 new consultation requests in your inbox. Your calendar is booked 2 weeks out. You're raising your prices because demand exceeds your capacity.
>
> **Bridge:** The Client Attraction System is the exact framework our students use to go from invisible to fully booked in 90 days. No dancing on Reels required. [Book your free strategy call]

**BAB works because:** It leverages the contrast principle (Cialdini) — the gap between Before and After creates desire, and the Bridge provides the vehicle to close that gap.

### Framework: Post-Click Journey (Perry Marshall's Timeline)

**When to use:** After writing ad copy — because the ad is only the beginning of the conversion process. Perry Marshall's 80/20 principle: the post-click experience determines 80% of whether someone converts.

**The complete journey:**

```
Ad Click → Landing Page → Trust Building → Conversion → Follow-Up → Repeat Purchase
   ↑                                                              ↓
   └──────── Retargeting (non-converters) ←───────────────────────┘
```

**Stage-by-stage optimization:**

1. **Ad → Landing Page transition:** The first 5 seconds after click determine bounce or stay. The landing page headline must echo the ad's core promise verbatim. Visual continuity matters — same colors, imagery, energy as the ad.

2. **Trust building (above the fold):** Social proof (reviews, logos, badges), specific outcome numbers, professional design. Perry Marshall's rule: you have 8 seconds to prove credibility before they leave.

3. **Conversion mechanism:** Single CTA principle — one page, one action. Every element on the page should support that single action. Remove navigation, remove distractions, remove choice.

4. **Follow-up for non-converters:** 95-98% of clickers will not convert on the first visit. The money is in the follow-up: retargeting ads (3-14 day window), abandoned cart emails (1hr, 24hr, 72hr), SMS for high-intent actions.

5. **Post-purchase sequence:** Confirmation → onboarding → value delivery → review request → cross-sell/upsell. Each touchpoint reinforces the purchase decision and builds lifetime value.

**The conversation starts with the ad and continues through every touchpoint.** Inconsistency at any stage breaks trust and kills conversions.

### Framework: Landing Page Alignment

**When to use:** When building or auditing the post-click experience for any Meta ad campaign.

**Message match checklist:**
- [ ] Landing page headline echoes the ad's primary promise (word-for-word is ideal)
- [ ] Hero image/video matches or extends the ad's visual
- [ ] Offer details (price, discount, bonus) are identical to what the ad promised
- [ ] CTA button text matches the ad's CTA ("Get Free Guide" not "Submit")
- [ ] Tone and energy are consistent — casual ad → casual page, professional ad → professional page

**Mobile-first requirements (98%+ of Meta traffic is mobile):**
- Single-column layout — no side-by-side content that requires horizontal scrolling
- Tap targets minimum 44x44px — fingers, not cursors
- Font size minimum 16px body text — readable without zooming
- CTA button visible without scrolling on mobile viewport
- Forms: maximum 3-4 fields. Every additional field reduces completion by 10-15%

**Load speed imperatives:**
- Every additional second of load time costs approximately 7% of conversions (Google data)
- Target: under 3 seconds on 4G mobile connection
- Compress images, lazy-load below-fold content, minimize JavaScript
- Use Google PageSpeed Insights — aim for 90+ mobile score

**Trust signals above the fold:**
- Star ratings or review count ("4.8 stars from 2,340 reviews")
- Client logos or "as seen in" badges
- Guarantee badge ("30-day money-back guarantee")
- Security badges near payment forms

### Framework: Funnel-to-Campaign Mapping

**When to use:** When translating your funnel strategy into actual Ads Manager campaign structure. This bridges strategy and execution.

| Funnel Stage | Campaign Objective | Campaign Type | Targeting | Budget Share | Key Creative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TOFU** | Awareness / Video Views | Manual | Broad, 1-5% LAL, interest stacks | 35-40% | Educational video, brand story, problem agitation |
| **MOFU** | Traffic / Leads | Manual | Video viewers (50%+), engagers, site visitors (30d+) | 20-25% | Case studies, lead magnets, webinar invites |
| **BOFU** | Sales / Leads | Manual or ASC | Site visitors (7-14d), ATC, checkout, email list | 30-35% | Testimonials, urgency offers, risk reversal, DPA |
| **Post-Purchase** | Sales (cross-sell) | ASC or Manual | Past purchasers (exclude recent), loyalty segments | 5-10% | New product launches, upsells, referral programs |

**Structural rules:**
- TOFU and BOFU should be in separate campaigns — never mix cold prospecting and retargeting in the same campaign
- Use audience exclusions to prevent overlap: BOFU campaigns exclude TOFU audiences, MOFU excludes BOFU audiences
- ASC can blend TOFU and BOFU internally (use existing customer cap to control ratio)
- Each campaign should have its own naming convention prefix (TOF_, MOF_, BOF_)

**When to use:** As starting points for copywriting. Fill in the brackets, then refine for voice and specificity.

>
> [Product name] helps [target audience] [achieve specific outcome] without [common objection/friction].
>
> Here's what [number] customers are saying:
> "[One-line testimonial with specific result]" — [Name, Title]
>
> [Limited-time offer detail — discount, bonus, deadline]
>
> [CTA button text] →

**Template 2: Story-Driven (TOFU/MOFU)**
> [Number] years ago, I was [relatable painful situation].
>

>
> Then I discovered [key insight or framework — not your product yet].
>
> That one shift led to [specific measurable result].
>
> I put everything I learned into [product/resource name] so you don't have to spend [time/money] figuring it out the hard way.
>

**Template 3: Social Proof Lead (MOFU/BOFU)**
> "[Powerful one-line testimonial with specific numbers]" — [Name]
>
> [Name] isn't alone. In the last [timeframe], [number] [target audience] have used [product] to:
> - [Benefit 1 with specifics]
> - [Benefit 2 with specifics]
> - [Benefit 3 with specifics]
>
> [Risk reversal statement — guarantee, free trial, etc.]
>
> [CTA] →

**Template 4: Problem-Solution (MOFU)**
> If you're a [target audience] who [specific problem], this is for you.
>
> Most [audience] try to solve this by [common wrong approach]. But that doesn't work because [reason].
>
> [Product/resource] takes a different approach: [one-sentence methodology].
>
> The result? [Specific outcome with proof point].
>
> [Soft CTA — "See how it works," "Get the free guide," "Watch the demo"]

## Key Principles

- **The funnel is a system, not a collection of campaigns.** Each stage exists to feed the next. Optimizing one stage in isolation sub-optimizes the whole system.
- **Cold audiences need education, not offers.** Trying to sell to unaware audiences is the most expensive mistake in Meta advertising. Warm them first with TOFU content, then convert with BOFU offers.
- **Copy framework selection follows awareness level.** Unaware audiences need AIDA (heavy on Attention). Problem-aware audiences need PAS. Solution-aware need BAB. Product-aware need direct response. Most-aware need a CTA and a reason to act now.
- **The ad is the beginning, not the end.** Perry Marshall's 80/20 rule applied to ads: 80% of the conversion outcome is determined by what happens after the click — landing page, follow-up, and trust-building.
- **Message match is non-negotiable.** If the ad says "Free Guide" and the landing page says "Premium Resource — $47," trust is broken instantly. Every element post-click must reinforce the ad's promise.
- **One page, one action.** Landing pages with multiple CTAs, navigation menus, or competing offers dilute focus and reduce conversion rates. Single CTA principle always.
- **Mobile-first is not optional.** 98%+ of Meta ad traffic is mobile. If your landing page isn't optimized for mobile, you're losing the vast majority of your traffic.
- **Budget the funnel, not the campaign.** Allocate budget across the entire funnel system. Over-investing in BOFU while starving TOFU leads to audience exhaustion and rising CPAs.
- **Templates are starting points, not destinations.** Every template must be adapted with specific proof points, authentic voice, and audience-specific language. Generic copy converts generically — which is to say, poorly.
- **Test copy frameworks, not just copy variations.** A/B testing "10% off" vs "15% off" is incremental. Testing PAS vs BAB vs AIDA is structural. Structural tests yield larger performance gains.

## Decision Tools

### Funnel Stage Diagnostic

```
Which stage needs the most attention right now?

1. Check retargeting audience sizes:
   ├── Website visitors (7d) < 1,000? → TOFU is underfunded. Increase top-of-funnel spend.
   ├── Website visitors (7d) 1,000-10,000? → MOFU may need strengthening.
   └── Website visitors (7d) > 10,000? → BOFU should be performing. If not, diagnose post-click.

2. Check conversion rates by stage:
   ├── High impressions, low engagement → Creative/hook problem (TOFU)
   ├── High engagement, low leads → Offer/lead magnet problem (MOFU)
   ├── High leads, low sales → Nurture/close problem (BOFU)
   └── High sales, low repeat → Retention problem (post-purchase)
```

### Copy Formula Selector by Awareness Level

| Audience Awareness | Best Copy Framework | Lead With | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | AIDA (heavy Attention) | Curiosity, entertainment, pattern interrupt | Product mentions, pricing, CTAs to buy |
| **Problem-aware** | PAS | Their specific pain in their words | Jumping to solution too fast |
| **Solution-aware** | BAB | Transformation contrast | Generic claims without proof |
| **Product-aware** | Direct response / Social proof | Testimonials, results, risk reversal | Re-explaining the problem they already know |
| **Most aware** | CTA + incentive | Urgency, scarcity, exclusive offer | Long copy — they're ready, just give them a reason |
